# implementation-draft-2.md — batch 01

*Draft 2 of 3, written from a blank page after reading draft 1 once. Draft 1's job was
to get the batch's material onto the table; this draft's job is to make it argue. Three
deliberate changes from draft 1: the 13-of-34 engineering verdict stays in **¶2**,
where it is a fact about the engine, while the two ratified system laws move to **¶5**,
where they are the output of governance — draft 1 carried all of it in one paragraph and
the verdict arrived as a list; **¶2** now describes the persistence layer as a layer, as
conditions.md §2 requires, leaving **¶3** only the signed-rule half the user can read and
delete; and every paragraph leads with the claim it is trying to win rather than with the
findings it has to spend. Six numbered paragraphs, prose only.*

---

**1.** A shell that puts the right tool under your hand while your hand is already on the
object is solving the easy half of the problem. The hard half is deciding which few
moments justify speaking to someone who asked for nothing, and ACTIONS is that half, built
on a deliberately rigid shape: something sensed, one sentence, one verb inside a nested
pill, a right-click that means not now and costs the user nothing, and a palm-sized gear
box that unfolds from the sentence which just spoke. Whether that shape becomes Clippy is
settled at admission rather than in the mechanism, so admission was made countable. A
thousand ordinary computer habits went into the funnel and thirty-four actions came out,
each gate a written criterion instead of a preference: 482 were roughly daily, 219 could
name a trigger a Wayland desktop can actually sense, 209 cleared privacy, 40 earned
thanks, 39 earned a sentence, 34 survived being attacked. No stage was refilled to protect
a number. The killing also produced a boundary nobody had thought to write in advance —
files, learning and reading, and finance and shopping were wiped out completely, because
where the user is already holding the object the right answer is an OPTION. ACTIONS belong
where the hands are not.

**2.** The engine earns the right to know things by never going looking for them. A
detector is a pure function over streams that already arrive — the audio collector's
`pw-mon` watcher, the Hyprland event socket, the zsh bridge — and no detector owns a
timer, so the ceiling on what any of them can learn is whatever the launcher already
senses anyway. The persistence layer underneath is one breath long: an evidence store is a
counter, an eight-byte class hash, a running median, a small fixed-bucket histogram,
written beside the launcher's own state under `$XDG_DATA_HOME/waverunner/`. That honesty
has a price, published rather than hidden: thirteen of the thirty-four can speak on
today's engine. The twenty-one that cannot fail in three shapes, and the shapes matter
more than the count. Three wait on verbs tagged [new] — A02, A29, A37. The majority wait
on named [cheap] work one level deeper inside a collector that already runs: a title event
the parser currently drops, one more file inside a `read_dir` that already answers
`has_backlight`. Two, A26 and A33, are dormant on an absence rather than a gap, because
Golem ships no idle manager and no locker at all — and A35 escaped that same dormancy only
by re-specifying its trigger as four hours with no focus visit, off the focus stream alone.

**3.** The surface is not a proposal; it has been running since 2026-09-07, and every block
in the batch inherits its scale from that one live utterance. The current-task pill morphs
into *"The sun is set, do you want to turn on eye protection?"*, a nested amber **[turn
on]** fires `hyprsunset`, a right-click withdraws the offer, and the gear that grows out of
the sentence already carries the *Automatically at sunset* toggle — settings living inside
the action they govern, present only while the topic is alive. A38 is the proof that this
surface can grow without speaking more: it adds a second nested pill, **[and dim the
screen]**, to that existing sentence and writes no sentence of its own, ever. Its block
also produced the batch's only piece of layout doctrine, read off the morph code rather
than imagined: the width math hardcodes a single inner label, so where
sentence plus three pills would overrun the bar, the second verb is dropped rather than
truncated, because half a verb is worse than no verb and the pill must read identically on
a 2013 Air and a desk monitor. What the user can hold in their hands is ownership: a
standing rule is one readable line under `~/.config/golem/rules/`, and revoking it means
deleting that line, never rebuilding the system.

**4.** Privacy here is arithmetic, which is the only form of the promise that can be
checked. Thirty-three lifecycle rows — an id, the day last offered, the answer given, a
refusal count — come to roughly thirty-two bytes each, about 1.06 KB for the whole
register. The two largest per-action stores, A39's and A40's, are about 206 bytes; A38's is
two. Added up, the engine's entire memory of its user is a few kilobytes of counters,
hashes, medians and histograms, with no timestamped diary anywhere in it, because an
aggregate was available every time one was wanted. A36 shows what gestures-not-content
means in code: the clipboard module already refuses to record vault-born clips on the
strength of a declared `x-kde-passwordManagerHint` type, so the signal is the existence of
a type name rather than anything inferred from bytes. Nothing nameable sits at rest at all
— every site, command, file or window title a sentence needs is read live at the moment of
speaking. And one discipline turned up three separate times, which is why it is written
down as a law instead of trusted to care: never learn from your own hand. A17 counts only
the user's own do-not-disturb toggles, A38 must not count `battery_dim`'s own dimming, and
A39 must not increment the usage counter that is the dock's single sort key.

**5.** Admission is not a gate the list passes once; it kept killing after the list
looked finished. The first adversarial hunt attacked all
thirty-nine drafted actions and removed five against an empty bench, four of them verb or
admission failures the earlier cuts had no lens to catch. Governing thirty-four mouths that
share one user needed two laws no per-action rule could reach. The day's budget is a
priority queue rather than a ceiling — at most three sentences a day, never two inside an
hour, ranked by expiry instead of by type, a distinction A16 forced, a pending update being
a moment whose value does not die with the moment. The second is one mouth per cluster,
amended so that an answered member goes to the back of the queue; without it, a refusal is
data becomes a refusal is ignored. The ladder has edges now too:
for a habit the rule can be the first offer, but A13 fixed that at verbs which write a
rule, not verbs which assign a name, so the alias action ships with no automatic rung. Where another program owns the behaviour the verb flips that program's own switch, as
A35 and A37 do. Refusals escalate toward silence, and the notebook keeps every detector
listed, forgettable per line and revocable per detector.

**6.** The voice turned out to be the cheapest part of this, and the batch has evidence
rather than an opinion. The hunt ran a dedicated voice pass over all thirty-nine
drafted sentences and killed nothing; all five casualties were upstream, in admission or in
the verb. What decided each row is worth keeping as the working test: a fact about a thing
is an observation, a fact about a person is a verdict. *"Your microphone just went live"* is
the benchmark, and *"you keep going back and forth"* is admissible only because the
alternation being named is the cost about to be removed, not a flaw being pointed out. The
two repairs show where the real danger sits. A11's sentence was clean while its verb
moralised, so **[cap the volume]** became **[start quiet after midnight]** — a button label
is speech too, and the batch had been reading half its own output. A28's sentence
had to get longer rather than shorter, to name the headset microphone its verb takes away,
because a sentence that hides a cost is worse than one that is merely long. The feel that
falls out of all this is rarity: most of these actions go silent forever after a single
yes, and the safest of them adds capability while adding no sentence at all.
