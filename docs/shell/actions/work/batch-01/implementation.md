# implementation.md — batch 01

*The final of three independent passes (drafts 1 and 2 preceded it; each was written from
a blank page, not edited into the next). Six numbered paragraphs, prose only, coverage
fixed by conditions.md §2. Every number in it is a number the batch measured.*

---

**1.** Putting the right tool under a hand already reaching for it is the tractable half
of the problem, and that half is OPTIONS. ACTIONS owns the other half — which few moments
earn the right to address someone who asked for nothing — and answers it with one
unvarying shape: a sensed condition, one sentence, one verb inside a nested pill, a
right-click meaning not now and never punished, and a palm-sized config box grown from
the sentence that just spoke. Clippy did not fail because that shape is wrong, but
because everything was allowed to use it. So admission was turned into a count. A
thousand ordinary habits went into the funnel and thirty-four actions came out: 482 recur
roughly daily, 219 can name a trigger a Wayland desktop actually senses, 209 clear
privacy, 40 earn thanks, 39 find a sentence, 34 survive being attacked. Set the two middle
gates against each other and the economics show — privacy removed ten candidates, the
usefulness bar removed a hundred and sixty-nine. The scarce resource was never sensing,
never discretion; it was gratitude. The cutting drew a border nobody had thought to write:
files, reading and shopping went extinct entirely, because when the hands are already on
the object the honest answer is an OPTION. ACTIONS live where the hands are not.

**2.** The engine buys knowledge only from traffic it was already paying for. A detector
is a pure function over streams that arrive regardless — the audio collector's `pw-mon`
watcher, the Hyprland event socket, the zsh bridge — and no detector may own a timer, so
nothing in this register costs the machine a single new wakeup. Evidence is a layer, and a
short one to describe: a counter, an eight-byte class hash, a running median, a small
fixed-bucket histogram, written beside the launcher's own state under
`$XDG_DATA_HOME/waverunner/`. Holding that line costs coverage, and the cost is published
rather than buried — ten of the thirty-four can speak on today's engine. What matters
about the other twenty-four is not the count but the shape of the distance, because the
distance is shallow. Three wait on a verb that does not exist yet: A29, A34, A37. Most
wait on named [cheap] work one level deeper inside a collector already running — a title
event the parser currently drops, one more file read inside the `read_dir` that already
answers `has_backlight`. Two, A26 and A33, wait on an absence rather than a gap, since
Golem ships no idle manager and no locker at all; A35 left that same waiting room only by
re-specifying itself as four hours with no focus visit, which the focus stream answers
alone.

**3.** The surface is not a proposal — it has run since 2026-09-07, and every block takes
its scale from that one live utterance. The current-task pill morphs, never popping up or
seizing the pointer, into *"The sun is set, do you want to turn on eye protection?"*,
carrying a nested amber **[turn on]** that fires `hyprsunset`, and a right-click that folds
it away. The gear grows the sentence into a palm-sized box whose contents are ordered by
law: the parameters of the thing itself, then the conditions under which it was allowed to
speak, then its own lifecycle — five controls at the ceiling, because a box needing a tree
is a Settings app in costume. A39 shows the ceiling working. Seven absorbed habits are
spent as controls rather than recited: which applications are in the morning set, where
each one goes, run it at login, leave what is open alone, forget my mornings. A38 shows
the surface growing without speaking more — a second pill, **[and dim the screen]**, on a
sentence already on the bar, and where the width will not carry both, the second verb is
dropped rather than truncated, since half a verb is worse than none. What the box writes,
the user holds: one readable line under `~/.config/golem/rules/`, revoked by deleting it
and never by a rebuild.

**4.** Constitution VI opens with a claim about capability, not intent, so it can be
audited rather than believed. The `options-engine` manifest lists anyhow, thiserror, serde,
serde_json, tracing, tokio, zbus and libc: no HTTP client, no TLS stack, no URL type
anywhere in it. Tokio's `net` feature is on, because the engine's sockets are Unix sockets
and its bus is D-Bus, so the honest audit is narrow — nothing here can speak HTTP. Above
that floor, privacy is arithmetic. Thirty-three lifecycle rows — an id, a day, an answer,
a refusal count — are thirty-two bytes each, 1.06 KB for the register; the heaviest,
A02's, is ≈700 B, A39's and A40's ≈206 B fourth, A38's two. A user's entire trace is a
few kilobytes of
counters, hashes, medians and histograms, no timestamped diary, because an aggregate
answered every question asked. A36 is gestures-not-content: the clipboard module already
refuses to record a clip declaring `x-kde-passwordManagerHint`, so the signal is a type
name's existence, never a byte beneath it. Nothing nameable rests on disk; titles and
commands are read live at the moment of speaking. One discipline recurred three times, so
it has a name — never learn from your own hand: A17 counts only
the user's own do-not-disturb toggles, A38 must ignore `battery_dim`'s own forty per cent,
A39 must not touch the usage counter the dock sorts by.

**5.** Admission never stops; it is one question asked at three depths, and the lifecycle
is where the last two get asked. Existence is the first, and it went on killing after the
list looked finished: the hunt attacked all thirty-nine drafted actions and removed
five against an empty bench, four of them verb or admission faults the earlier cuts could
not see. The second is the day, which produced the two laws no
per-action rule could reach, because thirty-four mouths now shared one user. The day's
budget is a priority queue rather than a ceiling — three sentences at most, never two
within an hour, ranked by expiry rather than type, a distinction A16 forced: a pending
update is a moment whose value does not die with the moment. The other is one mouth per
cluster, amended so the answered member drops to the back of the queue, without which a
refusal is data becomes a refusal is ignored. The third is the single offer: the hand
before the rule, except where the verb writes a rule rather than assigns a name, the
boundary A13 fixed; the app's own switch where the app owns the behaviour, as in A35 and
A37; refusals escalating toward silence; and the notebook, every detector listed,
forgettable per line and revocable per detector.

**6.** The voice was the cheapest part of this, and the batch has evidence rather than an
opinion about it. A dedicated voice pass over all thirty-nine drafted sentences killed
nothing, while admission killed five — the register was never where the danger lived. What
decided each sentence is worth keeping as a portable test: a fact about a thing is an
observation, a fact about a person is a verdict. *"Your microphone just went live"* is a
thing; *"you keep going back and forth"* passes only because the alternation it names is
the cost the verb is about to remove, not a flaw being pointed at. Two repairs show where
the danger actually sits. A11's sentence was clean while its verb was not, so **[cap the
volume]** became **[start quiet after midnight]** — a button label is speech too, and the
batch had been reading only half of its own output. A28's sentence had to grow longer, not
shorter, to name the headset microphone its verb gives up, because a sentence that hides a
cost is worse than one that runs long. The feel that falls out of all this is rarity: most
of these go silent forever after a single yes, and the safest of them adds a capability
while adding no sentence at all.
