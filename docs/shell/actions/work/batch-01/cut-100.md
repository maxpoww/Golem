# cut-100.md — batch 01, F4

*The privacy cut, per conditions.md §1 (F4) and Constitution VI (five laws, three
disciplines). Killed: anything needing content-reading, cloud, or
surveillance-feel that cannot be reformulated as gestures-not-content and
aggregates-not-diaries. The test applied to every line was Law VI.4, the
disclosure test: imagine Golem saying to the user's face what it tracked and
why — if the honest disclosure feels like being watched, or the only sentence
the detector can ever produce is an indictment, the candidate dies here.*

**Result: 209 of 219 survive (10 killed).** The nominal "100" was never honestly
reachable, and no kills were manufactured to approach it (Constitution VII —
padding a kill list is slop too). The structural reason: F3 grounded every
survivor in an engine that is incapable of network, blind behind the browser
glass, and forbidden raw keys — so the classic privacy violators (browsing
diaries, content readers, cloud phone-homes, keystroke habits) already died at
F3 as *unsensable*. The architecture already was the policy; F4's honest job
turned out to be a focused hearing, not a massacre. The heavy killing now falls
to F5, whose usefulness bar is the real razor for the ~209.

**The five lines F3 ordered a hearing for — verdicts:** T0012 incognito
**killed**; T0214 bank counter **killed**; T0217 language detection **survives
reformulated** (shape only, no detection); T0002 title counter **survives under
name-at-offer-time** (below); T0089 commit-message shape **survives** (length
bucket only).

**Four doctrines established for P5+ (the action blocks must obey these):**

1. **Name-at-offer-time.** Detectors store hashes and counters at rest; every
   name a sentence needs (site, command, file, title) is read LIVE from the
   world at the trigger moment — the N+1th occurrence is the offer's material.
   Nothing nameable sits on disk. Applies: U0002, U0015, U0037, U0057, U0093,
   U0097, U0107, U0110.
2. **No stopwatch over leisure.** Duration-of-pleasure detectors (game overrun,
   autoplay past bedtime, evening that became an hour) are surveillance
   regardless of aggregate shape, and every sentence they can utter is a guilt
   trip — double-death under VI (feel) and II (voice); F4 gets them first.
3. **Compulsion counters are indictments.** A counter whose only possible
   sentence is "you do this too much" (inbox re-checks, distraction loops,
   slacking during work hours) cannot ever speak warmly. Killed as
   surveillance-feel, not deferred to F5.
4. **Transient classification is not content-reading.** The shipped clipboard
   classifier's shape checks (is_url, is_code, error-shaped, all-caps token,
   sentence-length, image-mime) read bytes to emit a CLASS and store only the
   class — same discipline as the title marker heuristics. This is the line
   between sensing and surveillance, and it is already how the engine ships.

Survivor schema: `U0001 (T-id S-id H-id) [tag] (category) text — T: trigger
[feasibility]` — U-ids sequential, lineage intact back to habits-1000.md.
`— P:` notes appear only where F4 imposed a reformulation or at-rest constraint.

## Casualties worth noting

- **T0012 incognito (S0042 H0048) — the flagship kill.** The user opened a window whose one meaning is "remember none of this" — and the detector's job would be to remember it. The disclosure test destroys it in one sentence: "I noticed you use private windows" is the exact betrayal the window exists to prevent. Sensing it politely is impossible; the polite system is blind to it BY DESIGN, and that blindness should be a stated feature of the engine.
- **T0214 bank-visit counter (S0461 H0881)** — the hearing F3 ordered; verdict: death. Even hashed, a morning-bank-visit counter is a record of financial attention — an intimate category tracked for a trivial benefit (a shortcut the user's fingers already know). Law VI.4: "I've noticed you check your bank every morning" is a sentence no OS should be in a position to say. With it dies the entire finance/shopping category — 60 habits entered F2, zero reached F5.
- **T0017 inbox re-check counter (S0055 H0067)** — doctrine 3's namesake. A per-day count of compulsive mail checks can only ever say "you check your inbox too much." That sentence is an indictment in every possible phrasing; a detector whose entire output space is guilt is surveillance wearing a wellness costume.
- **T0034 personal messenger during work hours (S0088 H0111)** — the workplace-slacking monitor. "Counter, not log" doesn't save it: the tracked FACT is "personal life on company time," and every offer built on it either moralizes or offers to hide the evidence. The OS must not be the kind of witness either sentence requires.
- **T0003 attention-lapse site cycling (S0012 H0012)** — needs to remember the identities of the 3–4 loop sites (a browsing diary in miniature, VI.2/VI.3) in order to diagnose distraction (surveillance of the user's attention). Two laws, one kill.
- **T0212 late-night aimless browsing (S0455 H0861)** — the sensing is live-state, but the habit's identity is a psychological diagnosis: "browsing NOTHING IN PARTICULAR rather than end the day." An OS that infers aimlessness has crossed from watching gestures into reading minds; no reformulation keeps the habit without the diagnosis. F3 asked whether any sentence avoids moralizing — F4 answers: none can, because the trigger itself is the accusation.
- **T0070 one-match-becomes-three (S0143 H0177)** — doctrine 2. The median-of-session-lengths is a sanctioned aggregate shape, but its only use is to hold a stopwatch over joy. "This was going to be one match" imputes a plan the user never stated. Parental-control energy; killed.
