# implementation-draft-1.md — batch 01

*Draft 1 of 3. P7 rewrites this from a blank page as `implementation-draft-2.md`,
then from a blank page again as the final `implementation.md`. Draft 1's job is to
get the batch's own material onto the table: every claim below should be traceable
to something `cut-50.md`, `actions-40-draft.md`, `slop-hunt.md` or `actions-40.md`
actually found, not to a restatement of the constitution. Six numbered paragraphs,
prose only, coverage fixed by conditions.md §2.*

---

**1.** Golem's shell already puts the right tool under the hand when the hand is on
the object; ACTIONS is the other half — the few moments the system speaks first,
having noticed something and formed an intention on the user's behalf. The shape is
fixed and small: a sensed condition, one sentence, one verb in a nested pill, a
right-click that costs nothing and means not now, and a palm-sized gear box that
grows out of the sentence that just spoke. The reason this is not Clippy is
arithmetic rather than taste. The batch began with a thousand ordinary computer
habits and ends with thirty-four actions, and every stage was a named criterion
rather than a preference: 482 survived roughly-daily, 219 could name a sensable
trigger on a Wayland desktop, 209 survived privacy, 40 survived the usefulness bar,
39 could be given a sentence anyone would thank the system for, and 34 survived the
first adversarial hunt. The funnel's own structural finding states the boundary
better than any rule written in advance: three whole categories — files, learning
and reading, finance and shopping — went extinct, because wherever the user is
already holding the object, the right answer is an OPTION. ACTIONS live where the
user's hands are not.

**2.** A detector is a pure function over streams that already arrive — the audio
collector's `pw-mon` watcher, the Hyprland event socket, the zsh bridge — and no
detector owns a timer, so what it may know is bounded by what is already sensed.
Two system laws were ratified, because no per-action law reaches the problem of
thirty-four mouths sharing one user. The day's budget is a priority queue rather
than a gate: at most three sentences a day, never two in an hour, expiring moments
always speaking and habit unlocks waiting indefinitely — and A16 proved the rank is
named by expiry rather than by type, a pending update being a moment whose value
does not die with the moment. The second law is one mouth per cluster, where an
answered member steps to the back so the cluster drains and then falls silent.
Thirteen of the thirty-four can speak on today's engine. The twenty-one that cannot
fail in three shapes: three dormant on work tagged [new]; the majority waiting on
named [cheap] work in our own tree, always one level deeper in a collector that
already runs — a title event the parser drops, one more file inside the `read_dir`
that answers `has_backlight`; and two dormant on an absence, since Golem ships no
idle manager and no locker at all.

**3.** The surface is not hypothetical: the sunset prototype has been live since
2026-09-07 and is the register, the scale and the choreography every block inherits.
The current-task pill morphs into *"The sun is set, do you want to turn on eye
protection?"*, a nested amber [turn on] fires `hyprsunset`, right-click withdraws
the offer, and the gear growing from it already carries an *Automatically at sunset*
toggle — config living inside the action it governs, existing only while the topic
is alive. A38 is the batch's proof that an action can ship as growth on an existing
action rather than as new speech: it adds a second nested pill, [and dim the
screen], to that utterance and writes no sentence of its own, ever. Its block
produced the batch's one piece of layout doctrine: the morph's width math hardcodes
a single inner label, so where the sentence plus three pills would exceed the
available width the second verb is dropped, never truncated, since a half-visible
verb is worse than no verb and the prototype must read the same on a 2013 Air as on
a desk monitor. Every block shares one ownership convention: signed standing rules
live one readable line each under `~/.config/golem/rules/`, revocation is deleting
that line rather than a rebuild, and evidence lives beside the launcher's own stores
under `$XDG_DATA_HOME/waverunner/`.

**4.** Privacy is stated as arithmetic rather than as promise, because the batch can
add it up. Thirty-three lifecycle rows — an id, the day last offered, the answer, a
refusal count — at roughly 32 bytes each come to about 1.06 KB; the largest
per-action stores are A39's and A40's at about 206 bytes; A38's is two. The engine's
entire memory of its user is a few kilobytes of counters, hashes, medians and
histograms, with no timestamped diary. A36 is the model of gestures-not-content: the
clipboard module already refuses to record vault-born clips, recognised by a
declared `x-kde-passwordManagerHint` type, so the flag is declared rather than
inferred and the whole signal is the existence of a type name, never its bytes.
Nothing nameable sits at rest, because name-at-offer-time means every site, command,
file or title a sentence needs is read live at the moment of speaking. The batch met
one discipline three separate times, which is why it deserves a law rather than
care: never learn from your own hand. A17 counts only the user's own do-not-disturb
toggles, A38 must not count `battery_dim`'s own dimming, and A39 must open a morning
set without incrementing the `usage.json` counter that is the dock's single sort
key — or the engine learns its own behaviour and offers it back as the user's.

**5.** Admission is not a gate passed once; the funnel is the governance, and it
kept working after the list looked finished. Hunt #1 attacked all thirty-nine
drafted actions and killed five against an empty bench, so every kill lowered the
shipping number and nothing was padded to protect it — and four of the five were
verb or admission failures the earlier cuts had no lens for, which is the argument
for hunting the draft rather than trusting it. The ladder has real shapes now. For a
habit action the rule is the first offer, since the user's repetitions already
climbed the first rung; but the boundary found at A13 is that this binds when the
verb writes a rule, not when it assigns a name, so the action that would mint shell
aliases ships with no automatic rung: an engine authoring a user's vocabulary is
authorship without consent. Where another program owns the behaviour, the verb flips
that program's own documented switch, and the action does not exist for a program
that has none. A refusal is data that escalates toward silence: an answered offer
goes to the back of its cluster, and three refused evenings retire A38's second pill
silently. The notebook closes the loop —
every detector listed with its evidence, forgettable per line, revocable per
detector.

**6.** The voice was never the hard part, and the batch has evidence for that rather
than an opinion. The hunt ran a voice pass over all thirty-nine drafted sentences
and killed nothing; all five kills were upstream, in admission or in the verb. The
test that decided each row is worth keeping as the rule: a fact about a thing is an
observation, a fact about a person is a verdict. *"Your microphone just went live"*
is the benchmark, and *"you keep going back and forth"* only survives because the
alternation named is the cost being removed and not a flaw being pointed out. Two
repairs show where the danger actually lives. A11's sentence was clean while its
verb moralised — [cap the volume] became [start quiet after midnight], because a
label is speech too. A28's sentence had to grow longer, not shorter, to name the
headset microphone its verb takes away, since a sentence that hides the cost is
worse than a sentence that is merely long. The feel that follows from all of this is
rarity: thirty-four actions that mostly fall silent forever once accepted, and the
safest of them, A38, adds capability while adding zero new sentences.
